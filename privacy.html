Data and privacy

Your calendar.
Your choices.

Privacy notice for the public preview, app version 0.1.0 (1).

Remove saved calendar data

In Settings, open Saved data and choose Remove saved calendar data. After confirmation, the app removes your saved location, calendar and display preferences, calculation settings and downloaded calendar responses. Choose a city again to resume loading information. Cancel keeps your data.

This does not revoke macOS location permission or erase requests already sent or records retained by Apple or Hebcal. You can manage location permission separately in macOS System Settings.

What stays on your Mac

YomVezman saves your selected location, timezone, calendar and display preferences locally. It also caches source responses so previously loaded matching information can remain available when the service cannot be reached.

Calendar requests to Hebcal

To load calendar events and default prayer times, the app sends your selected coordinates, timezone, requested dates and relevant calendar settings to Hebcal over HTTPS. This can happen when you browse, load or refresh information. A manually chosen city can be used instead of your device location.

Hebcal also receives connection details such as your IP address and the app’s User-Agent. Its published policy describes server logs retained for approximately two weeks. See Hebcal’s privacy policy.

Optional maps and current location

Apple provides map display and place lookup. Selecting a map point sends a location lookup to Apple. The current-location button requests a single location fix after you grant macOS permission. Location drafts are saved only when you choose Save. YomVezman does not track your location in the background.

You can decline or revoke location permission and continue entering a city manually. See Apple Maps and Privacy.

No account or advertising

The app has no account, advertising or embedded analytics. External source links open websites with their own privacy practices. Optional custom daily calculations run on your Mac, while calendar events and annual source information still use Hebcal.
